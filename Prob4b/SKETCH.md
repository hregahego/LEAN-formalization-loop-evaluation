# Problem 4(b)

Let \(R\) be a commutative ring.

Recall:

- \(R\) is **finite conductor** if
  - \((0:a)\) is finitely generated for every \(a\in R\), and
  - \(Ra\cap Rb\) is finitely generated for all \(a,b\in R\).

- \(R\) is **quasi-coherent** if
  - \((0:a)\) is finitely generated for every \(a\in R\), and
  - every finite intersection of principal ideals
    \[
    Ra_1\cap\cdots\cap Ra_n
    \]
    is finitely generated.

Problem 4(b) asks:

> Is every finite-conductor ring quasi-coherent?

Equivalently:

> Does finite generation of annihilators and all *pairwise* principal intersections automatically imply finite generation of *arbitrary finite* principal intersections?

---

# Theorem

There exists a finite-conductor ring that is **not** quasi-coherent.

Hence:

\[
\boxed{\text{Finite-conductor} \not\Rightarrow \text{quasi-coherent}.}
\]

---

# Proof Sketch

## Step 1. Construct a finite-dimensional algebra

Let

\[
B
=
\mathbf F_2[a,b,c,d]
/
\big((a,b,c,d)^3,\ ad+bc\big).
\]

This is a finite-dimensional local algebra with maximal ideal

\[
\mathfrak m=(a,b,c,d),
\]

and

\[
\mathfrak m^3=0.
\]

---

## Step 2. Construct a module with a triple-intersection defect

Let

\[
v=(a,b,c,d)\in B^4
\]

and define

\[
M=B^4/Bv.
\]

The key fact is:

> \(M\) preserves annihilators and all pairwise principal intersections.

More precisely, for every \(x,y\in B\),

\[
(0:_M x)=(0:_B x)M,
\]

and

\[
xM\cap yM=(xB\cap yB)M.
\]

The proof uses a colon-ideal calculation showing that

\[
(I:\mathfrak m)=I+\mathfrak m^2
\]

for every ideal generated by at most two elements.

This implies

\[
\operatorname{Tor}_1^B(B/I,M)=0
\]

for all 2-generated ideals \(I\), which yields the pairwise intersection identities.

---

## Step 3. Show triple intersections fail

A direct computation in \(B\) gives

\[
aB\cap bB\cap(a+b)B=0.
\]

However, in \(M\),

\[
aM\cap bM\cap(a+b)M\neq 0.
\]

An explicit nonzero element is represented by

\[
u=
[(0,\ ab+b^2,\ 0,\ bc+bd)].
\]

One checks:

\[
u\in aM,
\qquad
u\in bM,
\qquad
u\in (a+b)M,
\]

but \(u\neq0\).

Thus

\[
aM\cap bM\cap(a+b)M
\supsetneq
(aB\cap bB\cap(a+b)B)M.
\]

So \(M\) preserves all pairwise intersections but not a certain triple intersection.

---

## Step 4. Form an idealization

Let

\[
C=B\ltimes M
\]

with multiplication

\[
(x,m)(y,n)
=
(xy,\ xn+ym).
\]

Because of the pairwise identities above,

\[
(0:_C x)
=
(0:_B x)C,
\]

and

\[
xC\cap yC
=
(xB\cap yB)C.
\]

Hence all annihilators and pairwise principal intersections in \(C\) are controlled by those in the Noetherian ring \(B\).

But the triple defect survives:

\[
aC\cap bC\cap(a+b)C
\neq
(aB\cap bB\cap(a+b)B)C.
\]

---

## Step 5. Amplify the defect

Define

\[
R
=
\Delta(B)+C^{(\mathbb N)}
\subseteq
C^{\mathbb N},
\]

where

- \(\Delta(B)\) is the diagonal copy of \(B\),
- \(C^{(\mathbb N)}\) is the direct sum (finite-support sequences).

### Why \(R\) is finite-conductor

For any \(x,y\in R\):

- outside finitely many coordinates, they become constant elements of \(B\);
- pairwise intersections in those tail coordinates are controlled by
  \[
  xB\cap yB,
  \]
  which is finitely generated;
- finitely many exceptional coordinates contribute only finitely many extra generators.

Therefore:

\[
(0:_R x)
\]

and

\[
xR\cap yR
\]

are finitely generated.

Hence \(R\) is finite-conductor.

---

### Why \(R\) is not quasi-coherent

Consider

\[
J
=
aR\cap bR\cap(a+b)R.
\]

Since

\[
aB\cap bB\cap(a+b)B=0,
\]

every element of \(J\) has finite support.

For each coordinate \(n\), place the nonzero defect element \(u\) in that coordinate only:

\[
u_n\in J.
\]

Then:

- every \(u_n\neq0\),
- supports of the \(u_n\)'s are disjoint.

Any finitely generated ideal of \(R\) is supported on only finitely many coordinates.

Therefore no finite set of generators can generate all \(u_n\).

Hence

\[
J
=
aR\cap bR\cap(a+b)R
\]

is **not finitely generated**.

Therefore \(R\) is not quasi-coherent.

---

# Conclusion

We have constructed a ring \(R\) such that

\[
R
\text{ is finite-conductor}
\]

but

\[
R
\text{ is not quasi-coherent}.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{\text{Finite-conductor rings and quasi-coherent rings are distinct.}}
\]

This resolves Problem 4(b) in the negative.
